# Explanation plan: [the idea]

Fill this in before writing an explanation, lesson or walkthrough. Then write the explanation in the order of the sections under "The explanation". Delete this planning block from what the reader gets.

- The reader: [who, and what they can already do]
- Words they already use for this: [their terms]
- First term they will not know: [term]
- What they should be able to do afterwards: [an action or a judgement, something observable]
- A wrong model they may hold: [the belief, or "none known"]
- The shape of the idea: [sequence | parts and connections | quantity changing | rule with cases | cause and effect]
- Second representation that carries that shape: [picture | table | chart | worked numbers | control to move]
- Level: [newcomer: worked steps in line | experienced: result and rule first, steps on request]

## The explanation

### 1. What it is for

[One or two sentences: the idea at its most general, tied to something the reader knows, and what they will be able to do.]

### 2. A concrete case

[One real case with names and numbers, solved or walked through step by step, each step with its reason.]

### 3. The same case, stripped down

[The case again with incidental detail removed: a diagram, a table, or the pattern with the case's values still in it. Same order and labels as the case.]

### 4. The rule

[The general statement. Every term in it points back to something in the case.]

### 5. Where it stops

[A near miss: a case that looks the same and is not, and what it lacks. If an analogy was used, where it breaks.]

### 6. Your turn

[A question the reader answers by using the idea on a new case, or a prediction to make before trying something. Answer placed after a break or behind a disclosure.]

### 7. In one line

[The rule or model in a sentence, in the terms just built, and the one next thing to try.]

## Before sending

- [ ] Every new term is defined where it first appears.
- [ ] No more than about four new things before they are used.
- [ ] The second representation uses the same names as the text and sits beside it.
- [ ] Every picture has a text equivalent.
- [ ] The reader has something to do.
